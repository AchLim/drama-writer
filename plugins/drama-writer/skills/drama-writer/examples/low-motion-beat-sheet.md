# Example: a 65-second low-motion beat sheet

An original episode for `references/low-motion-tension.md`. One room, one lead, one phone. Nobody runs; the tension is a message that cannot be unsent. Beat 3 and beat 5 are 10-second beats because each turns on a before-and-after change.

```markdown
# THE SECOND KEY - EP 01: Read at 2:14 (low-motion beat sheet)
Language: en · Runtime: 65 s, 5 beats · Format: vertical 9:16
Set and light: small apartment kitchen, night, one cold phone glow, rain on the window, no other lamp.
Cast (max 2 on screen): INES (34, black hair tied back, grey cardigan, wedding ring on left hand) · Voice: low, tired alto
Narrator (optional, off-screen): none
Reference images (max 6): INES face, INES cardigan, kitchen, phone. (Daniel appears only as a name on screen.)
Episode brief: hook = a message already marked "Read" that she never opened. Final image = the front door lock turning. Next question = who has the second key.

| Beat # | Timecode | Visual/Subtext Action | Spoken/VO Dialogue | SFX & Audio Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:15 | Close on the phone screen in INES's still hand: a message from her husband, marked "Read 2:14", sent to her number, which she has not opened / her thumb hovers, does not touch / end state: phone face up, her eyes on it | INES (VO) [low, tired alto, slow pace, fear held flat]: "I never read this. Someone did." | [SFX: slow clock ticking] / [SFX: rain on glass] |
| 2 | 0:15-0:30 | INES at the table, medium, the glow on her face / she turns the phone face down, then back up / subtext: she wants it to be a mistake / end state: phone up, screen waking | INES (VO) [low, tired alto, slower, hope wearing thin]: "Daniel is in Lisbon. The message says he is home." | [SFX: phone screen buzz] / [SFX: rain] |
| 3 | 0:30-0:40 | FF: INES reading, jaw loose, almost calm / LF: lips parted, breath stopped, face lit blue / a second message lands and her eyes drop down the screen | INES (ON-CAM) [whispered low alto, halting pace, rising dread]: "Who else is in this house?" | [SFX: fridge hum cuts out] / [SFX: single floorboard creak @0:08] |
| 4 | 0:40-0:55 | Over her shoulder, the dark hallway and the front door, her head turning slowly toward it / she does not stand / end state: eyes fixed on the door | INES (VO) [low, tired alto, very slow, certainty arriving]: "Two keys. I have one. He has the other." | [SFX: slow clock ticking] / [SFX: rain, low] |
| 5 | 0:55-1:05 | FF: the front door shut, handle still / LF: the handle lowered, door unopened / one slow movement | INES (ON-CAM) [flat low alto, barely voiced, cold]: "Daniel is in Lisbon." | [SFX: key sliding into lock] / [SFX: rain cuts to silence] |
```

Why it works:
- **Beat 1** hooks on a visual mystery and one VO line. The viewer wants to know who read it, with sound off.
- **70/30 by words:** about 27 words of VO against 10 on camera. The two on-camera lines are saved for the whisper and the last line, when the threat is real.
- **One lead, no third face:** Daniel exists as a name on screen, so the reference images stay at four of six.
- **Duration-aware beats:** the two 10-second beats are snaps (calm to dread, shut to turning), written as first and last frames. The 15-second beats hold the slow pressure.
- **Vocal direction on every line** with the same voice descriptor throughout. Nothing assumes an uploaded audio file.
- **Audio carries the story:** the clock is the deadline, the buzz is the truth arriving, the fridge cutting out is the room going wrong, the key is the cliffhanger.
- **The last beat** cuts on the inhale, with the handle moving and no reaction after it.
